# Unit 1 Learning Experience

## Status and priority

This is the current development priority. Public-site development is paused while HVAC Training Solutions defines, builds, and tests the structure and delivery of Unit 1.

No additional marketing pages, checkout, account, email, PayPal, automatic-enrollment, full-course conversion, or production work should proceed merely to stay busy. Planning may continue, but implementation returns to the public platform only after the Unit 1 learning pattern is accepted.

> If Unit 1 does not make a beginner feel capable, teach the approved material accurately, and make the learner want to continue, nothing else is ready to scale.

## Learning position

Young workers do not need a course disguised as social media. They need an experience that removes unnecessary friction and makes learning feel relevant, achievable, active, and connected to a real goal.

The experience should provide:

- immediate connection to HVAC work
- one clear concept at a time
- real equipment images, diagrams, and demonstrations
- learner-controlled pacing
- frequent low-pressure decisions and retrieval
- useful feedback instead of a score alone
- visible, meaningful progress
- bounded help at the moment of confusion
- Ron's instructor presence
- a clear connection to Interview Ready and supervised field learning

## Current risks to correct

- reproducing Moodle's read, watch, quiz sequence in a newer visual shell
- treating shortened text as sufficient engagement
- teaching definitions before showing why they matter
- presenting all 27 modules as one intimidating wall
- shrinking PowerPoint slides onto a phone
- using decorative or generic images instead of instructional visuals
- relying on long videos without learner control, captions, or a clear task
- using AI only as a chatbot after confusion has already grown
- letting AI invent content, answer keys, or completion decisions
- adding points, streaks, badges, or leaderboards that do not represent real competence
- waiting until course completion to connect learning to employment

## Source-content rule

The supplied Unit 1 course material, images, presentations, videos, checks, and instructor intent are the authoritative source for the pilot.

Before building:

1. Inventory every Unit 1 page, concept, image, presentation, video, question, and downloadable asset.
2. Identify the learning objective and HVAC relevance of each concept.
3. Mark duplicate, outdated, unclear, missing, or safety-sensitive material for Ron's review.
4. Preserve original meaning while redesigning delivery.
5. Do not substitute invented visuals or content when an approved original exists.

PowerPoint files should not be embedded as desktop slide decks. Extract the useful diagram, image, sequence, or explanation and rebuild it as a readable phone-first learning element. Record source and revision information so later updates remain traceable.

## Repeatable concept pattern

Unit 1 should prove one reusable pattern rather than create a different interface for every page.

| Step | Learner experience | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Why it matters** | A real HVAC situation, observation, or question | Establish relevance before theory |
| **See it** | An approved photo, diagram, short demonstration, or animation | Make the idea concrete |
| **Learn it** | One concise explanation in Ron's teaching voice | Build accurate understanding |
| **Try it** | Predict, identify, choose, order, compare, or explain | Make the learner active |
| **Get feedback** | Explain why the response makes sense | Correct the mental model |
| **Connect it** | Show where the concept appears in HVAC work | Support transfer to the trade |
| **Check it** | One low-pressure retrieval or application question | Confirm engagement and understanding |
| **Continue** | Save progress and present one obvious next action | Maintain momentum and resumability |

Not every concept needs every media type. Every element must earn its place by teaching, checking, or guiding.

## Session and mobile behavior

- A learning block should be short enough to complete in a normal phone session, but not shortened at the expense of understanding.
- The student controls the pace and can replay or reread an explanation.
- Progress saves automatically after meaningful actions.
- Leaving and returning restores the correct lesson and position.
- One prominent **Continue where you left off** action appears on the dashboard.
- Controls are touch-friendly and do not require pinching or horizontal scrolling.
- Videos include captions or an equivalent transcript where required.
- Images and diagrams include meaningful alternative text or an accessible equivalent.
- The interface avoids unnecessary animation, sound, and visual clutter.

## Active learning and worked examples

For a beginner, show a complete worked example before requiring independent problem solving:

1. Here is the situation.
2. Here is what Ron notices.
3. Here is what he would check or consider.
4. Here is why.
5. Here is the conclusion.

Then give the student a similar situation with less guidance. Interactions can remain simple: a choice, prediction, component identification, ordered sequence, reading interpretation, or brief explanation.

## Knowledge checks and soft gates

Unit 1 follows the approved no-hard-grading model:

- checks are frequent and low pressure
- feedback explains the concept, not merely correct or incorrect
- retries are unlimited and carry no penalty
- no letter grade or percentage grade is displayed
- an incorrect answer may lead to a hint, simpler explanation, worked example, or equivalent retry
- important concepts reappear later through spaced retrieval
- the Unit 1 gate requires completing approved checks, not earning an arbitrary average
- Ron can override a gate with a recorded reason

Approved questions and answer keys control progress. AI may explain or hint, but it does not decide completion.

## AI learning-help boundary

The lesson remains primary. AI appears only when useful and receives the minimum approved context for the current concept.

Allowed help includes:

- explain this more simply
- show another approved HVAC example
- explain why this matters
- give a hint
- quiz me on this concept
- answer a question about the current lesson
- identify repeated confusion and suggest an approved review
- escalate to Ron when the student remains stuck

The AI must not:

- wander across the unrestricted course
- invent procedures, readings, safety advice, or credentials
- complete a gated check for the student
- silently change an approved answer key
- decide that a learner completed a lesson or course
- replace the instructor relationship

AI failure must not block the lesson. Approved static explanations and normal course navigation remain available.

## Motivation without childish gamification

Meaningful progress statements should describe real competence, for example:

> You can now explain why a liquid changes shape while keeping its volume.

The experience may acknowledge genuine milestones such as Unit 1 complete or Interview Ready. It should avoid points for opening pages, forced streaks, cartoon rewards, and leaderboards.

Enjoyment comes from:

- understanding something that initially looked difficult
- seeing progress toward employment
- making successful decisions
- having control over pace and help
- recognizing real equipment and field situations
- knowing that a real instructor remains available

## Ron's instructor presence

Unit 1 should retain Ron's voice through concise introductions, field examples, common mistakes, short demonstrations, and clear explanations of why a concept matters. AI supports this teaching; it does not become the course personality.

## Unit 1 pilot test

Test the complete unit with three to five people resembling the intended beginner. They should use their own phones with minimal coaching.

Observe:

- where they hesitate or stop
- what they skip
- whether they understand the controls
- whether media helps or distracts
- which concepts cause repeated errors or help requests
- whether they can explain and apply the major concepts afterward
- whether they can leave and resume correctly
- whether they willingly continue
- whether the experience makes them feel capable of learning HVAC

Do not optimize primarily for time on page or number of clicks. The important outcomes are understanding, continuation, successful resume, useful help, and readiness for later course concepts.

## Unit 1 acceptance gate

Do not convert the remaining course until:

- every approved Unit 1 concept and source asset is accounted for
- every major concept has a clear HVAC reason and appropriate visual support
- the mobile lesson pattern is consistent and understandable
- worked examples and interactions teach rather than decorate
- checks and feedback are technically correct
- progress survives refresh, logout, and device change in the implemented pilot
- AI remains bounded and the lesson works when AI is unavailable
- accessibility and phone usability have been reviewed
- target learners complete the unit and can explain the important concepts
- Ron approves accuracy, tone, structure, and delivery

## Immediate sequence

1. Ron reviews the current site pages and authoritative Unit 1 content separately.
2. Inventory the complete Unit 1 source package and assets.
3. Map concepts, objectives, HVAC relevance, visuals, checks, and instructor notes.
4. Draft the Unit 1 page and interaction structure without importing the full course.
5. Build one representative concept and test the pattern.
6. Refine and complete Unit 1.
7. Test with target learners on phones.
8. Approve or revise the delivery pattern.
9. Only then reconnect the real sample to the public site and plan the remaining course conversion.
